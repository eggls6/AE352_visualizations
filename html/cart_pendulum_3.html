Forced, damped pendulum on a spring-mounted cart

Introduction

The pendulum cart is a useful model that can, among other things, illustrate the behavior of Skycranes that were pioneered by NASA to land heavy rovers on Mars. A pendulum of length l with bob mass m₂ hangs from a cart of mass m₁ that rolls on a horizontal track. A spring (stiffness k) and a dashpot (damping b) tie the cart to a fixed wall, and an external force F(t) = F₀ cos Ωt pushes the cart back and forth.

The system essentially consists of two coupled, constrained oscillators: a mass on a spring and a pendulum, trading energy through the pivot. The same model describes an overhead crane carrying a swinging load, a tuned-mass damper in a tall building, and loads that sway on moving vehicles.

Synopsis

  • Equations of motion. With the cart position x and the pendulum angle θ as generalized coordinates, the Euler-Lagrange equations give the two coupled equations shown at the top of the page. They are linear in the accelerations ẍ and θ̈, so each time step solves a 2×2 linear system and advances the state numerically.
  • Equilibria. The hanging state x = 0, θ = 0 is stable. The inverted pendulum θ = ±π is unstable and shows up as a saddle in the (θ, ω) portrait.
  • Small oscillations. Linearizing about the hanging state gives two normal modes. Their frequencies are the roots of m₁l ω⁴ − (kl + (m₁+m₂)g) ω² + kg = 0: a slow, cart-dominated mode and a fast, pendulum-dominated mode. The coupling pushes the two frequencies apart, so neither equals √(g/l) or √(k/(m₁+m₂)). The State panel shows both frequencies for the current parameters.
  • Damping. With b > 0 and no forcing, the motion loses energy and every trajectory spirals into the rest state, which is a stable focus. Only the cart is damped directly. The pendulum loses energy only through its coupling to the cart.
  • Forcing. Once the transients decay, a weak drive produces steady oscillation at the drive frequency Ω. Driving near either normal-mode frequency gives resonance, and the damping limits how large the response gets. At large amplitude the nonlinear terms (sin θ and the centrifugal term m₂l θ̇² sin θ) take over. The resonance then shifts in frequency, several different steady responses can coexist, and strong forcing makes the motion chaotic.
  • Reading the phase portraits. The full state (x, v, θ, ω) is four-dimensional, and the forcing also depends on t. Each portrait is therefore a slice of the 4D phase space: the flowing background visualizes the vector field with the other subsystem frozen at its current state. The bright curve is the real trajectory projected onto that plane. The slices change from moment to moment, which illustrates the coupling at work.
